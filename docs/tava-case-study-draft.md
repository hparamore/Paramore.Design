## Intro (target ~65 words)

Tava lets you switch therapists quickly, and it does that part well. The problem is
the people who never get that far — one or two sessions in, no rebooking, then
silence. They don't complain and they don't switch. They just stop. This is a concept
for the moment that catches them: a post-session check-in that turns a quiet exit into
a conversation.

---

## Section — The two ways people leave

Two failure modes, and only one of them is visible.

The first is switching. It works, but every switch costs a first session — you restart
your whole history with someone new, and the intake burns the hour you came for. That
came up constantly in research as the biggest complaint about changing providers.

The second is the one I wanted to solve: **people who just ghost.** They're the
majority, and the product never hears from them. The question isn't "why did they
leave?" It's **why didn't they rebook?**

---

## Section — Eleven directions, then two

I started in Whimsical with sketches and wireframes — about eleven directions for where
an intervention could live. I compared them all against the same four questions:

- **When** do you ask?
- **What** do you ask for?
- **How** is it framed?
- **Who** starts it — who surfaces this?

Two survived: a **concierge chat**, and a **post-session check-in**.

I flowcharted both. The chat puts a person or an AI between you and the problem, and
it surfaces prebuilt cards — here's a fix, here are people you could try, here are
open times. The complexity gets absorbed by whoever's on the other end.

The check-in is simpler and lands at the moment the feeling is still fresh. It ran
bigger as a flow, and after comparing the two side by side, it's the one I picked.

---

## Section — The part I'm proud of

Most of the time, leaving isn't about the therapist being wrong. It's about one thing
that never got said.

So the check-in offers to say it for you. **"What would you like them to know?"** — a
few starter prompts, or write your own. Then, before anything sends:
**"Here's exactly what we'll send."** You see the message, you can edit it, you can
back out.

**It isn't anonymous, on purpose.** If a provider has one client, anonymity is a
promise you can't keep — so don't make it. What Tava can offer instead is mediation:
it comes from Tava, on your behalf, and there's a paper trail. That's a real thing to
offer, and it's honest.

---

## Section — And if you do want someone new

The switch path assumes the thing that makes switching expensive: starting over.

You pick from a short list of providers, then choose what goes with you — past notes,
context, whatever you want them to have. Your next therapist starts already knowing
something about you, so session one isn't spent rehashing. Then date, time, summary,
and it's on your calendar.

---

## Section — Details

Reset buttons, toggles, and motion studies you can replay to see how each transition
should feel — because "does this feel gentle or clinical?" is the whole question in a
flow like this, and it can't be answered in a static frame.
