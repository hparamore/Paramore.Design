# Product

## Register

brand

> This is a portfolio — the design *is* the product. Every surface is a work
> sample. Craft is not decoration here; it's the argument.

## Users

Hiring managers, design leads, and design directors evaluating Hunter Paramore
for a design role. They arrive skeptical, time-poor, and pattern-matching fast —
they've seen a thousand portfolios and can smell a template in two seconds. Their
job-to-be-done: decide, quickly, whether this person has real taste, can ship
pixel-perfect work, and thinks design-first. Success = they reach out, or they
remember the site well enough to bring it up in a hiring conversation.

## Product Purpose

Prove hireability for a senior design role through the site itself, not just its
contents. The case studies show the work; the site's own craft shows the
designer. It exists to convert "another portfolio" skepticism into "this person
clearly has a point of view" within the first screen.

## Brand Personality

**Confident, bold, precise.** High-contrast and decisive — the pure-black canvas
and single orange accent are a stance, not a default. Voice is direct and
unhedged (mirrors the owner: "say it clearly, don't soften it"). The feeling in
the first five seconds is *certainty* — this person knows exactly what they're
doing. Never timid, never apologetic, never trying to please everyone.

## Anti-references

The site must NEVER read as any of these:

- **Generic SaaS / template** — centered hero → subhead → CTA, 3-up card grid
  with identical cards, soft drop shadows, the interchangeable Framer/Webflow
  look. The "AI-generated" tell. This is the primary thing to defeat.
- **Corporate / sterile** — cold, safe, agency-beige, no point of view. Could be
  anyone's site. Death for a portfolio whose whole job is to have a point of view.
- **Dribbble-overdesigned** — gradient mesh blobs, glassmorphism, trend-chasing
  effects that will look dated in six months. Bold ≠ trendy.
- **Cluttered / busy** — everything shouting at once, no breathing room. Bold is
  achieved through contrast and restraint, not density.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the work sample.** Hold every surface to the standard the case
   studies claim. Craft is the argument; sloppiness anywhere undermines it.
2. **Take a position.** Commit to bold, specific choices over safe, agreeable
   ones. A memorable wrong-for-some beats a forgettable fine-for-all.
3. **Bold through contrast and restraint, not volume.** Impact comes from one
   loud accent against deep black and generous space — never from piling on.
4. **Diverge from the default.** When a layout, type, or component choice feels
   like the obvious AI/template move, treat that as a signal to do something
   more deliberate. (See ART-DIRECTION.md.)
5. **Show, don't tell.** Demonstrate skill in the execution; don't describe it
   in copy. The polish should make the claims unnecessary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Dark-only theme; maintain WCAG AA contrast (≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 large text)
  against `#0A0A0A`. Watch tertiary/muted grays on black — verify, don't assume.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for every animation (the orange glow, any
  reveals, hover motion) — provide a static or crossfade fallback.
- Scalable text, real focus states, semantic structure, and accessible labels
  are defaults, not extras (per the owner's standing standard).
